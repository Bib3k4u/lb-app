1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the complete functional and non-functional requirements for LIMITBEYOND, a multi-tenant fitness and gym management platform. It establishes the agreed baseline against which the system will be designed, built, tested and accepted.

The platform replaces and substantially extends an existing working application, limitbeyond2.0, which today covers member and trainer administration, workout logging, an exercise library, diet chat, feedback and basic check-in. LIMITBEYOND is a ground-up rebuild that carries those capabilities forward onto a domain model designed from the outset for multiple gyms, multiple locations, scheduled classes, structured attendance and commercial operations.

1.2 Product Scope

LIMITBEYOND is operated by a single platform owner who onboards, activates and oversees many independent gyms, each running one or more physical locations. It serves four constituencies through a single mobile application with role-specific dashboards, supported by a web console for administrative work that is impractical on a phone:

  • Gym operations — member and trainer lifecycle, location management, membership plans, payment records, dues and renewals, reporting.
  • Training delivery — workout plans and logging, an exercise library, personal training packages, group class scheduling and booking across yoga, Zumba, meditation and conventional strength formats, delivered on-site or online.
  • Member outcomes — attendance, progress tracking, nutrition plans, analytics and a two-way feedback relationship with trainers and administration.
  • Engagement and intelligence — notifications, outbound WhatsApp messaging and AI-assisted planning, all delivered in later phases on top of the same core.

The commercial target is the Indian market: INR pricing, GST-compliant receipts, and a footprint of roughly 10 to 50 gyms. Multiple gyms are supported operationally from Phase 1; a gym becomes live when the Super Admin verifies it and records its subscription payment.

Two characteristics distinguish the model and shape much of what follows. First, a location is a strong boundary: two locations of the same gym have their own trainers, their own members and their own data, and an administrator granted authority over one holds none over the other. Second, every account is also a trainee: platform owner, gym owner, location administrator and trainer all train themselves, so each owns the same personal fitness profile and training dashboard a member does, independently of the operational authority they hold.

1.6 Conventions

Every requirement carries a stable identifier of the form FR-XXX-nn (functional) or NFR-XXX-nn (non-functional). Identifiers are permanent: superseded requirements are marked withdrawn rather than renumbered, so that test cases and architecture references never break.

Shall denotes a binding requirement. Should denotes a strong recommendation that may be traded away with the product owner's agreement. Priority follows MoSCoW, and each requirement names the phase in which it is first delivered.

On the word "Admin". Where a requirement says an Admin without qualification, it means an account holding admin authority over the location the action concerns — a Gym Owner anywhere in their gym, or a Location Admin at a location they administer. Where a capability is reserved to one tier, the requirement names that tier explicitly. FR-ORG-12 lists what is gym-wide and therefore the Gym Owner's alone.

On "location". This document says location where a gym would ordinarily say branch. They mean the same physical site; the term is used consistently to keep the isolation boundary in view.

2. Overall Description

2.1 Product Perspective

LIMITBEYOND is a new, self-contained product. It is not a module of a larger system and it does not depend on any incumbent gym software. It does consume external services — messaging, video conferencing, push notification delivery and, in later phases, an AI provider — but each of those is an optional adjunct: the core operational system shall remain usable when any external service is unavailable.

The product is delivered as a single mobile application that presents every account its own training dashboard, plus the operational dashboards and navigation conferred by whatever role grants it holds and wherever they apply — alongside a web console covering the same backend for administrative work. The two clients are presentation choices over one identical set of capabilities and one identical permission model; no capability exists on one client that is denied on the other purely by virtue of the client.

2.2 Relationship to limitbeyond2.0

The predecessor system is a working application built on a document database with three roles (Admin, Trainer, Member), covering authentication with administrative activation, user and trainer management, trainer-to-member assignment, an exercise library organised by muscle group, workout templates and logging, QR check-in, administrator-recorded payments, a feedback loop, a free-text diet chat, and AI-generated workout suggestions.

LIMITBEYOND is a fresh rebuild, not an extension. The decision rests on three structural limits in the existing model that cannot be resolved incrementally:

  • The check-in record carries only a user and a timestamp. It has no concept of a location, a session, a check-in method, or a trainer's shift — so class attendance, geofencing and trainer utilisation have nowhere to live.
  • The payment record carries only a user, a duration in months and an amount. There is no plan catalogue, no session credits, no dues, no GST breakdown and no renewal lifecycle.
  • Nothing is tenant-scoped or location-scoped. Retrofitting isolation onto every existing record is a migration of comparable cost to a rebuild, with materially higher risk of cross-tenant leakage.

Functionality that is sound in the predecessor is carried forward as requirements in this document rather than as code. Appendix A maps each existing capability to the requirements that preserve it, so that nothing already working is lost by accident.

2.3 Multi-Gym and Location Model

The platform hosts many independent gyms from the outset. Each gym is a tenant; each tenant runs one or more locations. Every operational record belongs to exactly one tenant and, below that, to exactly one location.

The hierarchy is: Platform → Gym (tenant) → Location → People.

Location is a hard boundary. Two locations of the same gym maintain separate trainers, separate members and separate operational data. A trainer at one location does not appear on another location's roster; a member at one location does not appear in another's member list; attendance, timetables, capacity, class bookings and payment records are all properties of a location. A Location Admin granted authority over one location holds no authority whatsoever over a sibling location of the same gym.

What remains genuinely gym-wide, and therefore the Gym Owner's alone: the gym profile and branding, the plan catalogue and pricing, the list of locations themselves, and consolidated reporting across locations. A member may be granted access to sibling locations, but this is an explicit grant recorded against their record, never a default.

2.4 The Verification and Activation Chain

No gym and no account becomes operational by self-service alone. Each is admitted by a human one level above it, and this chain is the platform's primary defence against fraudulent gyms and unpaid access.

1. Gym registersA prospective owner signs up, submits the gym profile, its locations and business details. Gym state: Pending Verification.
→
2. Super Admin verifiesPlatform operator reviews and approves or rejects with a reason. Gym state: Verified — but not yet live.
→
3. Super Admin activatesSubscription cash is collected offline and recorded against the gym with a term. Gym goes Live; the registrant becomes Gym Owner.
→
4. Staff & members sign upTrainers and members self-register, selecting the gym and one specific location. State: Pending Verification.
→
5. Admin verifiesThe Gym Owner, or a Location Admin for that location, approves each one. Account becomes Active; the journey begins.

Verification and activation are deliberately separate steps. A gym may be a legitimate business (verified) and still not be entitled to operate on the platform because its subscription has not been paid or has lapsed (not live). Until a gym is live, its trainers and members can neither register nor be verified. Until a person is verified, their application is restricted to a holding state: they may view and correct their own profile and see their pending status, and nothing else.

Beyond this chain, administrative authority is delegated laterally. Any admin may promote an existing trainer or member into a Location Admin for a named location, and that promoted admin may in turn promote others for locations within their own authority. The chain is permitted to extend, and is made safe by scope limits, last-admin protection, Super Admin override and a complete audit trail rather than by forbidding delegation. See FR-AUTH-16 to FR-AUTH-28.

2.5 The Dual-Facet Model: Operational Role and Personal Profile

Everyone who works in a gym also trains in it. Owners lift, managers attend yoga, trainers follow their own programmes and track their own progress. A model in which only members can have a workout history is wrong about how gyms actually work, and forces staff to keep a second account to train under.

LIMITBEYOND therefore separates two independent things that are commonly conflated:

  • The operational role — what authority an account holds over other people's data. Super Admin, Gym Owner, Location Admin, Trainer or Member, scoped to a gym and a location.
  • The personal fitness profile — the account's own training life. Workouts, routines, progress, measurements, photographs, diet plan, class bookings and personal attendance.

Every account owns a personal fitness profile, unconditionally. It is not a role to be granted, not a permission to be checked, and not contingent on holding a paid membership. A trainer sees the same training dashboard a member does; a gym owner logs their own sets against their own routine; the Super Admin tracks their own progress. Operational dashboards are then layered on top according to role, which is why an Admin's application shows both a training view and a management view.

Two consequences follow, and both are specified in M21. Staff train without holding or paying for a membership plan, so their personal activity never appears as revenue or plan consumption. And because a trainer arriving at a location may be starting a shift, doing their own workout, or both, attendance records the purpose — otherwise trainer utilisation reports inflate and footfall figures stop meaning anything.

3. User Classes and Characteristics

The platform recognises five operational roles. The Admin role of version 1.0 has been split into two tiers — Gym Owner and Location Admin — because administrative authority is now delegable and must be confinable to a single location. Front-desk receptionist and a nutritionist distinct from the trainer remain excluded: admins absorb front-desk duties, and both trainers and admins author nutrition plans.

RoleWho they areOperational responsibilities and privilegesScope
Super Admin The platform operator. A very small number of accounts, provisioned directly. Verifies gym registrations; collects subscription cash offline and records it; activates, suspends and reinstates gyms; overrides admin appointments in any gym; configures the global class-type and exercise catalogues; views cross-gym health and growth metrics. Holds no routine access to an individual's health records or progress photographs. All gyms
Gym Owner The admin who registered the gym. Commercially motivated, accountable for the whole business. Everything a Location Admin can do, at every location of their gym, plus the gym-wide matters reserved to them: the gym profile and branding, the plan catalogue and pricing, creating and closing locations, appointing and removing admins anywhere in the gym, and consolidated cross-location reporting. Location Admin A trainer, member or hire promoted to run one site. Typically a floor or branch manager. Verifies trainers and members at their location; manages that location's staff, members, timetable, attendance and payment records; assigns trainers to members there; authors plans and issues coaching feedback; may promote others to Location Admin for locations within their own authority. Cannot set pricing, alter the plan catalogue, create locations, or see any sibling location's data. Named location(s) only
Trainer Coaching staff — strength trainers, yoga and Zumba instructors, meditation leads. Phone-first, often mid-session. Views their assigned member roster; builds and assigns workout and nutrition plans; conducts and logs PT sessions; leads group classes and marks class attendance; records their own shift attendance; issues coaching feedback; reviews member progress and adherence. Assigned members at assigned locations
Member Gym members across all levels of fitness experience and digital confidence. The largest population by two orders of magnitude. Registers and maintains their profile; checks in; books and attends group and online classes; follows assigned workout and nutrition plans; raises feedback; views plan status, dues and receipts. Self only
Every role is also a trainee. The table above describes authority over other people's data only. Independently of it, every account — Super Admin, Gym Owner, Location Admin, Trainer and Member alike — owns a personal fitness profile and sees the same training dashboard: their own workouts, routines, progress, measurements, photographs, diet plan, class bookings and attendance. Staff need no membership plan to hold one, and may be assigned a trainer and coached like anyone else. See Section 2.5 and module M21.
On holding several roles at once. Roles are additive rather than exclusive. The commonest case, and an explicitly supported one, is Trainer + Location Admin: a trainer promoted to run a site who carries on coaching, keeping their assigned members, their classes and their shifts while gaining admin authority over that location. An owner who instructs a yoga class is the same pattern. The account accumulates the union of its privileges evaluated per location — never the maximum applied everywhere — and the application presents the corresponding dashboards together, each labelled, rather than forcing a context switch. A second account is never required. See FR-AUTH-26 to FR-AUTH-28.

8. Phased Delivery Plan

Scope is complete in this document; delivery is sequenced. Each phase is independently useful — a phase that ships alone still leaves the gyms on the platform better off than before it. The ordering follows the operating reality that a gym must first be able to admit, charge and track its members before it can schedule classes for them.

Changed in v1.1. Multi-gym operation, the two admin tiers with delegated promotion, the gym subscription lifecycle, and the universal personal fitness profile have all moved into Phase 1 — they are foundational to the identity and isolation model and cannot sensibly be retrofitted. Phase 5 is correspondingly reduced to self-service and scale conveniences, and the payment gateway has left the plan entirely.

PHASE 1 — Foundation, Multi-Gym Onboarding and Core Operations
Objective
The Super Admin can onboard and activate multiple gyms, and each of those gyms can run its daily operation across its locations entirely on the platform: admit people, take money, know who came in — while everyone on it, staff included, tracks their own training.
Scope
Multi-gym tenancy with location isolation · phone-OTP authentication · the full verification and activation chain · gym subscription offerings, recorded cash payments, activation, lapse and suspension · the five-role model with Gym Owner and Location Admin tiers · admin promotion, demotion, scope limits and last-admin protection · the universal personal fitness profile and training dashboard for every role · member and trainer profiles · trainer assignment · plan catalogue and memberships · offline payment recording, dues and GST receipts · QR and geofenced check-in with shift-versus-workout purpose · role dashboards scoped by location · in-app and push notifications · audit trail and consent.
Modules
M1, M2, M3, M4, M5, M6, M15, M19, M20, M21; the Phase 1 subset of M16.
Acceptance
Two or more gyms are registered, verified, paid for in cash and made live by the Super Admin; each runs at least two locations; trainers and members register against one location and are verified there; an admin promotes a trainer to Location Admin for one location and that admin is demonstrably unable to see the sibling location's data; a plan is sold, a payment recorded and a receipt issued; members and trainers check in by both QR and geofence for a full week, with shift and personal-workout attendance distinguished; a Gym Owner, a Location Admin and a Trainer each log their own workout and see their own progress without holding a membership; a gym's subscription is allowed to lapse and the grace-period behaviour is observed; every dashboard reconciles against the desk's own figures.
PHASE 2 — Training, Nutrition and Member Outcomes
Objective
The platform becomes the medium of coaching, not merely of administration.
Scope
Exercise library carried over from limitbeyond2.0 · workout templates, routines and set-level logging · personal records · PT packages, credits, scheduling and session notes · structured diet plans and the consultation thread · progress tracking with weight, measurements and photographs · two-directional feedback and coaching notes · announcements and the unified inbox · membership freeze · data export and erasure.
Modules
M8, M10, M11, M12, M13, M14; remaining items of M3, M5 and M15.
Acceptance
A trainer builds a routine and assigns it; the member logs workouts against it and records measurements; a PT package is sold and its credits decrement correctly on completed sessions; a diet plan is authored after consultation and revised with history preserved; coaching feedback reaches the member.
PHASE 3 — Classes, Scheduling and Online Delivery
Objective
Yoga, Zumba and meditation become first-class scheduled offerings, on-site and online.
Scope
Class type catalogue · recurring per-location timetables and session generation · booking with capacity, waitlist and auto-promotion · cancellation cutoffs and late cancellation · class rosters and attendance · no-show handling · trainer substitution and double-booking prevention · online sessions over external meeting links with timed link release and join tracking · location closure calendars · class packs · session ratings.
Modules
M7, M9; class-related items of M6, M14 and M2.
Acceptance
A weekly timetable generates sessions across locations; members book, join a waitlist and are promoted automatically on a release; a session is cancelled and every booked member is notified and restored; an online yoga class runs end to end with attendance recorded in-platform.
PHASE 4 — Analytics, Engagement and AI
Objective
The platform starts telling the gym what to do, and starts reaching members where they already are.
Scope
Full reporting suite — revenue, attendance, class performance, membership and churn — with filters and exports · trainer utilisation and remuneration reporting · at-risk member worklist · WhatsApp outbound messaging including habitual gym-time nudges and re-engagement prompts · AI workout progression, diet drafts, progress narratives and churn indications, all human-approved · nutrition adherence logging and the Indian food database · bulk member actions · spoofing review.
Modules
M16, M17, M18; deferred items of M12, M13 and M6.
Acceptance
Reports reconcile with ledger and attendance data; WhatsApp reminders deliver against approved templates with status recorded and daily caps honoured; an AI-drafted diet plan is edited and approved by a trainer before the member ever sees it, with the approval logged.
PHASE 5 — Self-Service and Scale
Objective
Reduce the Super Admin's manual effort per gym as the platform grows. Multi-gym operation already works from Phase 1; this phase makes it cheap to run.
Scope
Self-service gym onboarding with document upload and verification workflow · per-gym branding and subdomains · subscription renewal reminders and self-service renewal requests · cross-gym benchmarking for the Super Admin · usage limits per subscription offering · bulk gym administration tooling.
Modules
Extensions to M1, M2, M16 and M20.
Acceptance
A gym completes onboarding end to end with the Super Admin only approving and recording payment; gym and location isolation are demonstrated under a standing test suite; the Super Admin manages a subscription renewal cycle across a dozen gyms without per-gym manual reconstruction.
No payment gateway, in any phase. Version 1.0 placed member online payments in Phase 5. That is withdrawn. Member fees are collected offline by gym staff and recorded; gym subscriptions are collected in cash by the Super Admin and recorded. The platform is a book of record at both levels and never touches funds. Should this change, it is a new requirement against a later version of this document, not a deferred item of this one.

RSK-08Member adoption of the applicationIf members do not install it, check-in and engagement features are stillborn. Mitigate by making check-in the single most frictionless action in the product, and by retaining a staff-marked fallback.
RSK-09AI output quality or costUnreliable suggestions erode trainer trust. Mitigated structurally by mandatory human approval (FR-AI-03) and graceful degradation (FR-AI-08).
